Short loops, separate sensing returns

A4 moves 18 components to shorten the sensitive paths. Each GaN bootstrap leg is now 1.53 mm; VCC bypass drops from 5.45 to 2.59 mm. The bias switch-to-inductor path drops from 11.94 to 3.66 mm. Those paths stay on top copper without via transitions.

Rectifier VSS has a dedicated source-sense route, separate from the gate-current return. Local return planes, copper spreading and parallel return vias support the switching loops. Section labels make the board easier to identify at the bench.

These are measured geometric changes, not measured electrical performance. The retained winding model still estimates 2.85 W of DC copper loss before AC and core losses.

Trace the circuit directly

A6 connects C4 directly between HB and HS; SW and HS are joined inside the GaN IC. The readable VIN/AGND spacing, VD1/VG1 routing, vertical C11, test pads, J2 lead order and R16 placement are retained. D1 shows its unidirectional polarity.

Electrical correction: R16 connects LM5164 RON to PGND, as required by TI. This retains the grounding correction introduced in A3 hardware. Earlier prototype files are superseded. Review details.

The current-sense rectifier and filter feed OC through a direct wire. Timing, feedback injection and soft start use separate branches. The bias buck, ripple network and 5 V regulator are wired together locally; secondary sense and supply connections are wired directly too.

Supply arrows meet their rails and visible IC pins meet their symbol bodies. Aligned reference/value fields and separate capacitor ratings keep the drawing readable. Enlarged sections and native PDF/SVG exports have been reviewed. Text-to-wire clearance, pin attachment and four-way-junction checks pass.

Compact, with bench access

Opposing 5.08 mm headers accept removable screw-terminal plugs. Bench leads can be changed without soldering a harness. Six bare probe pads provide VIN/PGND, VOUT/SGND and V5/AGND measurement pairs.

Four M3 holes reuse the flyback example's exact exposed-substrate edge footprint: 3.2 mm drill, 6.4 mm mask opening and 10 mm exclusion on all copper layers. The compact board uses a 55 × 47 mm pattern, different from the flyback's spacing.

Pin 2 is positive and pin 1 is return on both power ports. Mark the mating input and output plugs: the identical connector family does not prevent a cable swap.

Headers overhang the PCB. Plugs, wire bend radius, removal space and the underside ferrite add to the assembly envelope. Renders show unmated headers; the area reduction describes the PCB only.

Look through the winding stack

The 4:2:2 transformer has repeatable PCB windings, paralleled secondary layers and a stock ELP22 core. It needs no bobbin or hand-wound coil. The tradeoffs include eight heavy-copper layers, filled vias, termination loss and coupling/capacitance that still require measurement.

Parts and assembly

$23.68 electronics + $0.39 mating plugs + about $6.88 core/clips = $30.95 parts allowance. Catalog snapshots exclude PCB, assembly, setup, attrition, minimum quantities, tax and shipping.

The board headers are C3697; two matching C71370 screw plugs are external accessories, not PCB placements. All 75 purchased PCB parts map to 41 stocked catalog codes in the snapshot. L2 has only 39 units in that snapshot.

Checked CAD, unqualified hardware

ERC, DRC, connectivity, schematic parity, pin-net mapping and BOM/CPL agreement pass. Independent geometry checks screen winding contacts and junctions. A clean-directory rebuild reproduces the routing and pad geometry.

First-harmonic calculations are a screening model, not a switching simulation. The 60 W target still needs magnetics, startup, no-load behavior, current limit, OVP, ZVS, synchronous rectifier timing, control-loop and thermal validation. Functional isolation only.
